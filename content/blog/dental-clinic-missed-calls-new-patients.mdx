A new patient with a cracked tooth searches "dentist near me", calls the first clinic, and gets voicemail. They call the second. That second clinic just won a patient who might have stayed for years. It happens constantly, not because clinics don't care, but because the front desk is busy with the patient in front of them.

## Why new-patient enquiries get missed

- **The front desk is juggling.** Checking in patients, handling payments and insurance, answering the phone. Something gives.
- **Peak call times clash with peak clinic times.** People call at lunch and after work, exactly when the clinic is busiest or closed.
- **Messages pile up.** Instagram DMs, WhatsApp, website forms and Google messages arrive in different places, and nobody owns all of them.
- **After-hours pain.** Toothaches don't keep office hours. An enquiry at 9 PM often won't wait until 9 AM.

## What a missed enquiry costs

A new patient isn't one appointment. It's a check-up, treatment, hygiene visits and often family members, over years. Losing even a few new-patient enquiries a week adds up to a serious amount of lifetime revenue. Try the numbers for your clinic with our [ROI calculator](/tools/roi-calculator).

## What good looks like

The clinic that wins is the one that replies first with something useful:

1. **Within a minute**, day or night.
2. **Acknowledges the problem**: "Sorry you're in pain."
3. **Asks what matters**: symptoms, urgency, new or existing patient, insurance (US) or treatment interest (e.g. aligners, implants).
4. **Offers real slots** and books one.
5. **Hands off** anything clinical or sensitive to the team.

Here's a realistic example from a US clinic:

> **Patient (8:47 PM):** Do you take new patients? Cracked a molar and it's starting to hurt
>
> **Clinic:** Yes, we do, and sorry you're in pain. Is it constant or only when you chew? And do you have dental insurance?
>
> **Patient:** Only when I chew. Delta Dental PPO
>
> **Clinic:** We're in-network with Delta PPO. We have an urgent slot tomorrow at 8:30 AM, or 3:15 PM.

## Three ways to fix it

### 1. Better processes (free)

- One person owns the inbox each shift
- Forward calls to a mobile when the desk is busy
- A same-day callback rule for every voicemail

This helps, but it still depends on someone being free.

### 2. An answering service

A human answering service catches calls, but usually takes messages rather than booking, costs more per call as you grow, and doesn't cover Instagram or WhatsApp.

### 3. An AI receptionist

An AI assistant answers every call, text and message in under a minute, 24/7. It asks your intake questions, books into your schedule, sends reminders, and passes anything clinical to your team. It should:

- Introduce itself as the clinic's assistant, never pretend to be a dentist
- Never give medical advice beyond what you approve (e.g. "rinse with warm salt water")
- Escalate emergencies and anything it's unsure of, straight away
- Keep patient details private and logged in your own systems
